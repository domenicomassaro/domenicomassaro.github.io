The Governor

You lead the central bank of an economy initially in equilibrium. Over thirty periods, you set the interest rate: your task is to keep inflation at 2%.

period 1 of 29

How it works

You are the governor of the central bank. The economy starts in medium-run equilibrium: output and natural output are both 100 (index), inflation is 2%, and the real interest rate is 2%. Over 29 decisions, you must keep inflation as close as possible to the 2% target while responding to shocks.

The IS–LM–PC model

IS (reduced form)Y = 100 + θ − 0.9(r − 2) LM (central bank choice)r = real interest rate chosen by you natural outputYn = 100 + s output gapgap = Y − Yn Accelerationist PCπt = πt−1 + 0.1 · gap

The IS curve describes the negative relationship between output and the real interest rate; the horizontal LM curve indicates that the central bank chooses the rate. Holding shocks constant, raising the rate reduces output; lowering it increases output. The 2% rate is the reference rate that keeps output at its natural level in the absence of the effects of shocks: it is not always the stabilising rate.

For simplicity, you choose the real interest rate directly, rather than the nominal rate. Output is represented by an index: 100 is the initial level, not 100 euros or the actual GDP of a country. The gap is the difference between two indices, expressed in index points, not a percentage calculated by dividing by natural output.

The PC with adaptive expectations implies that inflation rises when Y exceeds Yn and falls when Y is below Yn. When Y = Yn, inflation remains constant, but does not automatically return to 2%. Bringing inflation back to target after a deviation may require a temporary nonzero output gap.

Shocks

Demand shocks shift the IS curve through θ. Supply shocks are interpreted, as in the course, through changes in firms' markups: a higher markup reduces natural output Yn; a lower markup increases it. In the game, the shock changes Yn directly, without simulating a specific markup equation.

Shocks arrive in predetermined periods that you do not know in advance. Their effects persist and decay by 40% in each subsequent period. Research department reports signal only the arrival of new shocks: no new shock does not mean that the effects of previous shocks have disappeared.

The game screen

Research department reports
Signal new demand impulses and new changes in markups, which affect natural output.
Your decision
You observe inflation, the output gap, and the previous interest rate, then set the real interest rate for the new period. You can use the number field, slider, or shortcut buttons. Rates can be set in increments of 0.05 percentage points and can be negative.
Research department hint
Provides an approximate interval containing the rate that would close the output gap and therefore keep inflation constant. If inflation is already away from 2%, that rate will not necessarily bring it back to target. The game counts how many times you view the research department's hints.
Period outcome
Shows the output gap, output index, and new inflation rate.
Charts
Track the output gap, inflation relative to target, and your chosen interest rate relative to the 2% reference rate over time.
At the end
You receive an assessment of your term, summary indicators, charts comparing your decisions with a benchmark policy, an estimate of your interest-rate reaction rule, and a data table. You can also end your term early.

How performance is measured

Your loss is the mean absolute deviation of inflation from the 2% target: a lower value means better performance. You are compared with a benchmark policy of perfect stabilisation that starts at 2%, closes the output gap in every period, and therefore keeps inflation at target throughout. This is an ideal benchmark: it knows the shocks exactly, whereas you receive less precise information.
